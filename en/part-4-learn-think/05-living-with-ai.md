# Living with AI

Four dimensions of life, a skill stack, focus and rest.

> **Original language:** Traditional Chinese. This English version is a translation. If the two versions disagree, the [Chinese original](../../zh-TW/part-4-learn-think/05-living-with-ai.md) is authoritative.

## 1. Become a Digital Renaissance Person

**Digital Renaissance Person** is a term from notes on the video The Digital Renaissance: someone with basic competence across many areas of life, depth in one chosen craft, independent judgment, and AI as leverage. The three stances toward AI are in [AI Fundamentals §2](01-ai-fundamentals.md#2-where-ai-fits-a-motorcycle-for-the-mind).

## 2. Develop four dimensions of life

Borrowing the four quadrants of Ken Wilber's integral theory, life can be split into four dimensions:

| Dimension | What it includes | What to focus on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mind (individual interior)** | Thoughts, emotions, beliefs, interpretations | Critical thinking and self-awareness |
| **Body (individual exterior)** | Health, behavior, habits, appearance | Energy and steady physical training |
| **Spirit / Connection (collective interior)** | Relationships, culture, meaning, belonging | Meaningful connection and reflection |
| **Vocation / Society (collective exterior)** | Career, education, economics, contribution | Useful skills, work, and financial stability |

Think of it as a skill tree in a video game: put every point into one attribute and weaknesses show up everywhere else. Career success does not remove the need for health or relationships, and reflection does not solve money problems.

## 3. Build a distinctive skill stack

A skill stack has three layers:

1. **Understanding people:** psychology, writing, communication, marketing, public speaking, and sales. Understanding problems, attention, and how people perceive value.
2. **Your field, passions, and particular interests:** design, fitness, philosophy, coding, or nutrition, for example. Your combination of interests forms a distinctive point of view.
3. **Technical execution:** software, coding, design tools, automation, websites, and platforms. Using tools to turn ideas into finished work.

AI makes many tool-specific tasks easy, which raises the relative value of choosing problems and of domain expertise (a prediction; see [Thinking with AI §9](03-thinking-with-ai.md#9-separate-predictions-from-established-facts)).

## 4. Choose one main craft

You can explore many interests, but point your work toward one coherent body of work. Use this prompt to find a direction:

```text
Act as a career and business strategist. Help me identify a craft at the intersection of my interests, demonstrated strengths, and problems people will pay to solve.

Ask me focused questions, then propose three directions. For each, explain:

- The likely audience
- A problem worth solving
- A potential paid product or service
- Skills I would need
- The smallest project I could complete in seven days

Challenge vague assumptions. Do not assume I already have an audience.
```

The Digital Renaissance notes end with:

> **Choose one craft. Build one thing. Improve it every day.**

## 5. Alternate focus and rest

Charles Darwin's routine is a common example: about three hours of focused work a day, split across two morning sessions, with the rest of the day for walking, reading, reflection, and family ([The Marginalian](https://www.themarginalian.org/2013/05/10/charles-darwin-daily-routine/)).

Both modes need time:

* **Narrow focus:** making, writing, coding, solving, finishing.
* **Open focus:** walking, reflecting, reading, observing, generating ideas.
